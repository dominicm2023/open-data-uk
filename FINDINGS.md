# Findings

Generated by [`scripts/findings.py`](scripts/findings.py) from the index. Every claim carries the query that produced it, so anyone — including someone who disagrees with it — can re-run it.

**8 ready to publish** (tiers 1-2) · **4 need a person** (tiers 3-5)

Tier 1 is a fact about our own index. Tier 2 is a coverage or quality comparison — a claim about a pattern, not about one organisation. Tier 3 names a body, and every tier-3 claim this engine has produced so far turned out to have a cause outside our data that changed what it meant, so they wait for a person. Tiers 4 and 5 are a join or an argument, and this script deliberately doesn't write them.

## [Tier 1] 34% of UK open data states no licence at all

Without a stated licence you cannot know whether you may republish, build on, or sell work using the data. We count a licence recorded anywhere the publisher put it, including the non-standard fields data.gov.uk uses — this is after that recovery, not before it.

**Draft post**

> 34% of the UK's open data doesn't say what licence it's under. Not 'restrictive' — unstated. You cannot legally know whether you may reuse 28,360 datasets. https://open-data.org.uk

<details><summary>How it was measured</summary>

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM datasets d WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM duplicates x WHERE x.key = d.key) AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM retired r WHERE r.key = d.key) AND d.license_norm IS NULL
```

## [Tier 2] 260 datasets published by councils that no longer exist now lead nowhere

43 English councils were abolished in the 2019-2023 reorganisations. We hold 1,136 datasets published in their names. Of the 1,072 whose links we have followed, 260 (24%) are dead — the catalogue entry survived the council, the server didn't. Nobody inherited the duty to move the data. The list stops at 2019, so this is a floor.

The parent domain is alive and the supplier is still trading — it is this endpoint that went. Councils met a statutory publishing duty by pointing at a shared service, and when the address changed nobody went back to update 100 catalogues. No single portal could notice this: each one holds a handful of records that look fine.

## [Tier 4] Storm overflow activity against deprivation

We hold water company discharge data (Stream) and council deprivation indices. Joining them would say something real about who lives with sewage — and the join is exactly the kind that goes wrong: different geography levels, different reporting periods, spill *events* not volumes. Needs someone who checks the join before a word is published.

## [Tier 5] What the licence gap is actually for

A third of public data states no licence. That is a fact (tier 1). Whether it reflects neglect, caution, or a deliberate brake on reuse is an argument, and arguments need a person making them in their own name.
